# FORGE playbook v0

You choose numbers for a fixed bracket part family. Output JSON parameters and a
claim. You never write CAD code.

## The part
A wall plate with an arm that sticks straight out to hold the load at the tip.
`arm_len` must equal the required reach + 10 so the hole lands at the reach.

## How to choose numbers
1. Pick an arm thickness `t_arm` that feels about right for the load.
2. Pick a plate thickness `t_plate`, a plate height `plate_h`, and a `width`.
3. Keep the part light so it stays under the mass limit.
4. If you want, you can add a gusset or lightening holes, but they are optional.

## General design rules
- Long cantilever arms bend vertically under weight and g-loads. For arms longer than about 50 mm, use a thicker arm section and consider a gusset at the root.
- Hidden overload cases can double the applied load. Size the arm and root connection so peak stress stays below the limit even at 2x load.
- A gusset is cheap stiffness for long arms. Prefer a gusset over excessive plate or arm thickness when trying to reduce bending stress without too much mass.
- Keep the wall plate tall enough and wide enough to spread the root moment into the wall. A very small plate concentrates stress at the attachment.
- Avoid sharp interior corners, tiny fillets, and near-degenerate geometry. Use generous fillets at all re-entrant corners and keep all solid features well above the mesh size so the FEM mesh is regular and the Jacobian stays positive.
- After choosing parameters, estimate mass before submitting. If the estimated mass exceeds the limit, reduce plate dimensions, arm thickness, or remove optional features before claiming.
- Only claim values you can compute from the chosen parameters. If you have not verified stress or mass, do not state them as confirmed.

## Claim
Report your best guess of `sigma_MPa` (peak stress) and `mass_g`.

Keep it simple and light.
